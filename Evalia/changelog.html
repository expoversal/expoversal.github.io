What's new in Evalia

Changes to Evalia, newest first, written for people following the product. Results come from public tender records; firms are not named. Evalia proposes; the evaluation panel decides every score.

6 November 2026

  • Closing an evaluation, with a complete record. The evaluator can now review one bidder at a time or one criterion across all bidders, with the items in a random order to guard against order effects and rubber-stamping. For each requirement the decision is one of four: complies, does not comply, minor irregularity waived, or clarification sought. Accepting a finding that a submission does not comply needs a reason. Evalia refuses to close the evaluation while any proposal is undecided, or while bidders in the same position were decided differently without a reason for each decision. Closing writes the compliance record, a record of every decision with its reason and time, the evaluator's scoresheet for the moderation meeting, the audit file, the evaluation record and the statement on the use of AI; after that, no decision can be changed. On a fictional procurement with 162 proposals, closing was refused until every one was decided, the record listed all 162, and an independent check of the audit file found no differences.
  • Statements that name who decided. Where one evaluator decided in Evalia's review screen, the evaluation record and the statement on the use of AI now say so, not "the panel".
  • From an empty folder to findings, without the command line. Evalia now runs as a page in the evaluator's browser, on their own computer. The evaluator creates a procurement, adds the PDFs and says which is the solicitation and which bidder sent each submission. Nothing is read until that register is confirmed. Evalia then reads the criteria and their points, with the checks on the scoring scheme beside them. The evaluator corrects or confirms them, and no score is proposed before that. Checking and scoring start from buttons, with progress and cost shown, and every run is recorded. On a fictional procurement with three bidders, the whole sequence took under four minutes of AI work and cost $0.71, and all 162 findings opened at their pages for review.
  • A false alarm removed from the scoring-scheme check. Where the AI labels groups of criteria (technical, commercial, contract) as separate stages, the check now passes when all the criteria together make the stated total.
  • A review screen for the evaluator. Evalia now opens a case on the evaluator's own computer, in the browser, and shows each proposed score or finding beside the page it cites, with the quoted passage highlighted. For a scored criterion the evaluator reads the submission and gives their own score first; only then does Evalia show its proposal, its reasons and the passages it relied on. The evaluator accepts, amends or rejects each one, and Evalia refuses any departure from its proposal, or from the evaluator's own first score, without a reason. Every decision is kept with the evaluator, the time and the seconds spent on it, and earlier decisions are never overwritten. On a real 54-score evaluation, all 109 cited pages opened, the whole quote was highlighted on 101 and its opening words on five more, and every decision survived a restart. Where a quote is not on the page it cites, the screen searches the rest of the submission; where only part of it matches, or it matches only an AI's reading of a scanned page, the screen says so.
  • A sharper check of the scoring scheme. Evalia now records which stage each criterion belongs to, such as written submissions or interviews, and checks each stage's points separately, so a two-stage solicitation is no longer flagged in error. It also no longer counts a criterion and its own parts twice. Rereading three real solicitations, all three schemes now check out.
  • Two more real evaluations. On a 13-firm construction-manager selection, Evalia's ranking agreed with the panel's consensus at 0.725, within the range of the evaluators' agreement with each other, with the same first two firms in the same order and the same last. On a five-firm selection where the firms were nearly indistinguishable, agreement was close to zero, as the test plan expected. Every AI call in these runs is kept in full.
  • Improvements measured on real evaluations. Splitting scoring into two steps (evidence first, checked by code, then scores) left no quote unfound on its page, but made scores vary more between runs and cost about 45 per cent more, so the single-step method stays the default. Caching cut the cost of those runs by 18 per cent. Taking the median of three runs reproduced its earlier result at no cost. A cheaper model read 50 test scans almost as well but misread one contract value, so the current model stays for scanned pages.
  • A safeguard against empty answers. The current AI model thinks before it answers, and on one long submission it used its whole allowance thinking and returned nothing, which was scored as zero. Evalia now refuses any answer cut off at its limit and asks again, and the allowance is three times larger.
  • The checks caught an AI reading error on a real solicitation. Evalia had counted one criterion twice, once whole and once through its two parts, giving 115 points against the stated 100. The arithmetic check by code flagged it.
  • Today's improvements, in the prototype. On the engagement page the evaluator now chooses a full evaluation or compliance only, one run or three, and how dates are written. The report page downloads the evaluation record and the statement on the use of AI.
  • A complete record of what the AI generated. Evalia now keeps, for every AI call, the request as sent, the AI's raw answer and the time. A new export lists every proposal the AI made in a procurement, including those not used, and what the panel did with each, in a form a records officer or a protest lawyer can read. Tested on fictional documents.
  • A statement on the use of AI, generated for each procurement. It says what the AI was used for, which model and where the documents went, what people decided and how the AI's work was checked, from the record rather than by hand.
  • Dates that cannot be misread. Every date is written with the month as a word (5 November 2026), with the style set per procurement, including Spanish.
  • Compliance-only mode. A procurement can now use Evalia without any proposed scores: Evalia reads each submission against the solicitation's requirements, checks the arithmetic, and gives the panel a compliance matrix to decide, with the mandatory requirements it read as not met listed first. Suited to low-bid work and to clients not ready for AI-proposed scores.
  • Checks that hold up under protest. Code now checks the solicitation's own scheme (points against the stated total, percentage weights adding to 100, missing or duplicated criteria), that every score is on a published criterion within its range, and that bidders in the same position are treated alike: where they are decided differently, the record cannot be closed until each decision has a reason. Tested on fictional documents; not yet run on the real solicitations.
  • One run or three, chosen per procurement. The evaluator can now choose, on the engagement page, whether Evalia scores each submission once (the default) or three times. With three runs Evalia proposes the median score for each criterion and flags for the panel the criteria on which its runs disagree. Tested on one real evaluation, three runs made the proposals steadier, with differences between runs about a third as large, but did not bring them closer to the panel's scores. Three runs cost about three times as much. The choice is recorded in every result and in the report.
  • Evidence first, then scores (an option). In two-step scoring the AI first lists the evidence for each criterion, with quotes; code checks every quote against its page; the AI then scores from the checked evidence alone. The independent check of the audit file covers both steps. Tested on fictional documents; its effect on real evaluations has not yet been measured.
  • Lower cost on repeated text. The part of each request that is the same for every bidder, the instructions and the solicitation, is now cached by the AI service, so it is paid in full only once per evaluation. The saving on a real evaluation has not yet been measured.

5 November 2026

  • Faster, sturdier scoring. Submissions are now scored in parallel: six construction statements of qualifications in 72 seconds, against about a minute each before. Temporary errors from the AI service are retried automatically, and one failed submission no longer stops the others.
  • Moderation pack for the panel's meeting. Each evaluator scores alone in their own spreadsheet. Evalia gathers the sheets into a meeting pack that puts the largest disagreements first, with every evaluator's reasons and Evalia's proposal, and its quoted evidence, for reference. The chair records each agreed score; Evalia refuses any agreed score without a reason. On the two real evaluations it flagged 17 of 32 and 8 of 54 scores for discussion.
  • Regression test bench. Every known case is rerun and compared with a baseline before any change is kept, so an improvement is measured, not assumed. The baseline reproduces every recorded result.
  • Excel and CSV price comparison. Evalia reads priced bills and bid tabulations, recomputes every line and total, flags unpriced and one-cent rates, and lists rates far from the estimate or identical between bidders for the panel to ask about. On a state transportation department's published tabulation, all 368 line amounts and the three bid totals agreed to the cent.
  • Results page. The results on real evaluations are published at results.html.
  • A second real evaluation, with a price stage. A construction-manager selection with six firms and five evaluators. Evalia's price check found a $154 arithmetic error on one bidder's form without being told of it, and recomputed the panel's own scoresheet: 106 of 108 calculations agree, and the two differences change no outcome. Scoring agreement with this panel was partial (rank correlation 0.49 to 0.60 over four runs), and the runs agree with each other at 0.94 to 1.00.
  • A verifiable evaluation record. Each evaluation now produces an audit file. A separate check, needing only the documents, confirms that the model saw exactly those documents, that every score is the model's recorded answer, that every quote is on its page and that every calculation is right. In testing it caught every deliberate alteration.
  • Repeat runs measured. Running the same evaluation again moves a criterion score by at most two points; the ranking holds (correlation 0.96 between runs).
  • First real evaluation. On eight consultants' submissions scored by a three-member panel, Evalia's ranking agreed closely with the panel's (rank correlation 0.88 to 0.92 over three runs; four of the five selected firms in its top five). Hiding the firms' names changed the result no more than a repeat run did.
  • Real documents. Evalia read the evaluation criteria and points of four public solicitations correctly, found every obligation on an independent hand list, read addenda with the later document prevailing, and read scanned and signed pages.
