Product Evalia, the local evaluation application: the procurement page and the review screen, in a web browser

Contact vfjhello@outlook.com
Standards WCAG 2.2 Level A and AA; Revised Section 508 (36 CFR 1194), chapters 3, 5 and 6; EN 301 549 v3.2.1, clauses 4 to 12
Evaluated by The Evalia team (self-assessment). No independent audit yet.

How Evalia was evaluated

Fact. The checks below were run on the fictional example procurement, with a stand-in for the AI model, in Chromium (Playwright). Commands and results are in the repository.

  • Automated scan. python scripts/a11y_scan.py runs axe-core 4.10.2 with its WCAG 2.0, 2.1 and 2.2 A and AA rules on ten states: the list of procurements, settings, a procurement seen by a moderator and by an evaluator, and the review screen, each in the light and dark themes. Result: no violations. A further scripted run scanned the review screen with a finding open, the keyboard list open, a gate and a score before the evaluator's own decision, and the side-by-side view. Result: no violations.
  • Keyboard. A scripted run accepted, undid and amended a finding by keyboard alone, opened and closed the keyboard list with Escape, and walked the tab order. Every control is a native button, link or form field.
  • Zoom and reflow. At a width of 700 pixels (200 per cent zoom on a 1,400-pixel screen) and at 320 pixels, no page scrolls sideways; wide data tables scroll within themselves, as WCAG allows for data tables.
  • Text spacing. With the WCAG 1.4.12 spacing applied, no text was clipped.

Not done. Evalia has not yet been tested with a screen reader (NVDA, JAWS or VoiceOver) or with speech input, nor by people with disabilities. An automated scan finds only some failures. Until these tests are done, every "Supports" below rests on the checks above and on review of the code, and should be read as provisional.

Out of scope of this report. The documents Evalia writes (Word letters, Excel workbooks, the HTML and PDF records) and the Windows installer were not evaluated.

Terms

  • Supports: the functionality meets the criterion without known defects.
  • Partially supports: some functionality does not meet the criterion.
  • Does not support: most functionality does not meet the criterion.
  • Not applicable: the criterion is not relevant to the product.
