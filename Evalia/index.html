AI reads every bid and shows its evidence. Your panel decides every score.

The defensible way to evaluate construction tenders: the AI reads and proposes, your panel decides, and the record proves a person judged — thorough where it counts, fast where you choose.

Evalia is software a public owner's evaluators run on their own computers. It reads the tender and every submission, checks each bid against the requirements, and proposes a score on each criterion, quoting the page it relies on. Each evaluator scores first, then accepts or amends the proposal with a reason.

Where Evalia fits

AI procurement suites help agencies draft solicitations, find vendors and manage evaluations in one product, and e-procurement platforms receive the bids. Evalia does one part in depth: the evaluation of submissions for construction. It can be used beside either.

It adds what the suites do not: a proposed score on each criterion, shown only after the evaluator has recorded their own; a choice of two review speeds, score-first by default and a faster lane where a criterion is lighter; every quote checked against its page by code, and the prices compared across bidders; and published tests of its proposals against the scores real panels gave.

Who it is for

Public owners

Cities, counties, agencies and development authorities evaluating construction and professional-services tenders.

Owner's representatives and consultants

Programme managers, project managers and cost consultants who evaluate bids on an owner's behalf.

Owners with strict data rules

Evalia runs on the owner's own computers and can use a model on the owner's own servers, so that no document leaves its network.

What the AI never does

  • It never decides a score or a compliance finding: a person does, and a change from its proposal needs a reason.
  • It never does arithmetic: code computes every sum, date, threshold and price formula.
  • When it reads and scores a bid, it never sees another bidder's submission or the panel's scores.
  • It never reads a price before the technical evaluation is closed.
  • It never sends a document to an AI provider or region that the procurement has not permitted.

The application, screen by screen

What these screens are. Screens of 20 November 2026. The tender and the bidders are fictional, generated by script. Evalia's findings on them were read by a live AI model on the evaluator's computer. The decisions shown were recorded for this demonstration and are not an evaluation.

1Every step, with its status

Each procurement opens on a list of its steps and where each stands, in words. Steps that cannot start yet say so.

2Register the documents first

The evaluator adds the solicitation and every submission, and says what each file is and which bidder sent it. Nothing is read until this register is confirmed. Price files stay sealed until the technical evaluation is closed.

3Confirm the criteria before any score

Evalia reads the criteria and their points, each traced to its page, and code checks the scheme: points against the stated total, criteria missing or counted twice. The evaluator corrects or confirms them; no score is proposed before that.

4Declare interests before seeing a bid

Each evaluator declares any conflict of interest with the named bidders before any submission is shown. A declared conflict stops that evaluator's review.

5On a gate, the evaluator decides first

For a mandatory requirement, the evaluator reads the submission and records a decision before Evalia's reading is shown.

6Then Evalia's reading, and its evidence

Evalia then shows its reading and the passage it relies on, highlighted on the page. Code has checked the passage word for word against that page. Departing from one's own first decision needs a reason.

7What each decision changed

After each decision Evalia says what it recorded and what it changed, such as a requirement now settled for every bidder. Any decision can be undone; both are kept in the record.

8One requirement, every bidder

The evaluator can open one requirement and see every bidder's finding beside its page. Findings can be accepted together only after each has been opened and ticked, with one reason for all.

9A blind check against rubber-stamping

One in five findings accepted together is drawn at random and decided again with Evalia's reading hidden. A check that disagrees reopens the others.

10Prices, checked by code

Once the technical evaluation is closed, code recomputes every priced line against the schedule issued. Here 24,200 times 180 is 4,356,000, not the 4,176,000 printed. The evaluator decides every price item; Evalia proposes no price adjustment.

11A panel that never sees each other's work

With several evaluators, a moderator gives each role its criteria. Each evaluator reviews alone, on their own computer, and returns a locked file. Colleagues' scores open only after one's own return.

12The evaluator's own judgement, at closing

Before closing, Evalia summarises the evaluator's judgement: decisions made before its reading was shown, amendments with reasons, blind checks. It shows no agreement rate and no speed.

13Closed, with a complete record

Closing writes the compliance record, every decision with its reason and time, the evaluation record and a statement on the use of AI. After that, no decision can be changed.

14Debrief letters, drafted without AI

Once the award is decided, Evalia drafts a letter for each bidder with its own scores and reasons, and only what the rules allow about the winner. Code refuses any letter that would name another bidder.

Features built

Everything below runs in the application and is covered by automated tests on fictional documents. Dated entries are in the changelog.

Foundations · Runs on the evaluator's own computer, installed in one step, in English or Spanish.
  • An industry-neutral engine, construction knowledge in a separate pack, and one gateway for every AI call that keeps documents where the client allows.
  • Runs as a page in the evaluator's browser, on their own computer: create a procurement, add the files, say which is which, without the command line.
  • Windows installer without administrator rights, with a fictional example procurement; the AI key kept in Windows Credential Manager; removal safe to repeat.
  • One look on every computer, with its own typeface and no network needed; each procurement page lists its steps and their status in words.
  • Accessibility checked against WCAG 2.2 AA in light and dark themes, by keyboard, at 200 per cent zoom and on narrow screens.
  • Screens in English or Spanish, chosen on each computer, with a short guide in Spanish; text from documents is never translated.
  • Screens in Arabic, right to left, as a draft awaiting a native speaker's review.
Reading the documents · Reads the solicitation, its addenda and every bid, and checks each quote and figure by code.
  • A document register, with a fingerprint of every file, confirmed by a person before anything is read.
  • Criteria, points and requirements read from the solicitation, each with a quote checked by code on its page; addenda read with the later document prevailing; scanned and signed pages read.
  • The scoring scheme checked by code: points against the stated total, weights adding to 100, criteria missing, duplicated or counted twice, stages scored separately.
  • Confirmed criteria stay confirmed when a re-reading is identical, and lapse when anything differs.
  • Responsiveness pre-check by code: documents and pages, page limit, required forms by name, addenda acknowledged by number in five languages, validity period.
  • Two versions of the solicitation compared sentence by sentence, each change with its page.
  • Sentences shared word for word between bidders flagged as an integrity indicator, leaving out wording from the solicitation and form text every bidder shares.
  • Pass/fail thresholds, such as a minimum turnover or a maximum safety rate, read from each submission with the passage and page.
  • Scanned pages read on the evaluator's own computer if the owner chooses, with low-confidence pages flagged; a quote on them is never treated as verified.
  • English, Spanish and Arabic documents (right-to-left text put back in reading order); numbers read the local way, and left unread rather than misread; dates written with the month as a word.
  • Addenda applied to the requirements they change: one an addendum removed is no longer checked, and a changed one is checked as it now stands.
  • Terms a firm accepts simply by submitting (its own costs, how long the offer stays valid, rules of conduct) listed once in the record, not checked against every bid.
Findings and proposed scores · Checks every requirement and proposes scores, each with its reasons and quotes.
  • Each submission read against every requirement, with the mandatory ones read as not met listed first; each finding labelled Fact or Interpretation.
  • Compliance-only mode: requirements, arithmetic and a compliance matrix, with no proposed scores.
  • Proposed scores with reasons and quoted evidence; one run or three, with the median proposed and disagreements between runs flagged.
  • Options: evidence first, checked by code, then scores; or evidence cited sentence by sentence through the AI provider's citation feature.
  • Submissions scored in parallel, temporary errors retried, answers cut off at their limit refused, repeated text cached to lower the cost.
  • Questions about the documents, answered only from quotes checked by code on their pages; an answer with no verified quote is withheld, and every question is logged.
  • A bidder's programme from Primavera P6 or Microsoft Project checked by code: logic, leads and lags, constraints, float, durations, dates and the completion date.
The evaluator decides · The evaluator decides first and gives reasons; accepting without reading is caught.
  • Each proposal shown beside the page it cites, quote first; the evaluator scores, and decides gates, before Evalia's view is shown.
  • Four compliance decisions, a reason for every departure, items in random order, one key per decision so agreeing is no quicker than disagreeing.
  • One requirement for every bidder side by side; findings accepted together must each be opened, and one in five is re-checked blind.
  • When an evaluator changes one in five readings of a kind, the rest of that kind are decided one by one; nothing learnt is carried to another procurement.
  • The screen shows what is left to decide and why the close is refused.
  • Blind scoring: firms' names hidden until the panel reveals them, with the reveal logged.
  • A declaration of interests before any submission is shown; a declared conflict stops that evaluator's review.
  • A clarification log for every exchange with bidders, prices included: a request drafted from verified quotes, then the reply and the outcome with a reason, each with its time; replies that offer a price change flagged.
  • A pre-check decision is shown on the same point in the review, as a person's record, before the evaluator's own decision; it decides nothing.
Prices · Recomputes every priced line and sets prices against the estimate and the published formula.
  • Priced bills and bid tabulations in Excel or CSV recomputed line by line; unpriced and one-cent rates, rates far from the estimate and rates identical between bidders listed.
  • Two envelopes: price documents sealed until the technical evaluation is closed, then checked against the schedule issued for items omitted, blank, marked included or altered, and provisional sums changed.
  • Exclusions and qualifications listed by the AI, each with a quote checked on its page; a levelling workbook in which every figure carries its source.
  • Lines added outside the bill reviewed one by one, with a clarification that cannot invite a change of price.
  • Adjustments for comparison recorded only by the evaluator, with basis and reason; the tendered price never changes.
  • Price points under the solicitation's own formula, with the working written out.
  • The owner's estimate with published analysis thresholds; quantity corrections applied to every bidder and the ranking recomputed; a price far below the others opens a request for an explanation, never a rejection.
  • Lines a bidder did not price shown with the other bids' and the estimate's figures at the tender quantity, for reference only.
  • A bid tabulation in the form public owners publish: totals as read and as recomputed, ranked on the totals as read.
  • Exclusions labelled by the evaluator by what they put at risk: life safety, structural, long-lead, interfaces and more.
A panel, kept apart · Several evaluators score apart and see each other's work only to moderate.
  • A moderation pack that puts the largest disagreements first; the chair cannot record an agreed score without a reason.
  • Several evaluators by role, each with a package holding only their role's documents and proposals, returned as a locked file.
  • Colleagues' scores and reasons opened only after one's own return is made.
  • For the chair, once every return is in: the spread of scores on each criterion, and the range of agreement between evaluators beside Evalia's.
Closing and the record · Nothing changes after the close, and the record explains every decision.
  • Checks that hold up under protest: every score on a published criterion and within its range; bidders in the same position decided alike, or the difference explained.
  • The evaluation cannot close while anything is undecided; once closed, nothing can change, whoever opens it.
  • An audit file that an independent check, needing only the documents, confirms: documents seen, answers recorded, quotes on their pages, arithmetic right.
  • Every AI request and answer kept, and an export of every proposal and what the panel did with it.
  • A statement on the use of AI for each procurement, naming who decided.
  • Debrief letters in Word for every bidder (US debriefing or UK assessment summary), built by code that refuses to disclose another bidder.
  • Retention dates and legal hold; Evalia never deletes anything. Pilots sealed until the award is recorded.
  • A closed evaluation exported in the Open Contracting Data Standard, the format many governments publish in, and published tender data read back in.
The owner's data stays with the owner · Documents go only where the owner allows, and Evalia refuses anything else.
  • Each procurement says where its documents may be processed; any other call is refused, never redirected.
  • An owner's own AI model, in its own country or on its own servers, as well as a hosted one.
  • Local server closed to other websites; every library pinned and matched against its fingerprint at installation.
  • A data-handling statement covering retention, legal hold and prices.
Training and testing · Planted errors train evaluators; a test bench checks every change.
  • Training procurements with planted errors, on fictional tenders only, report what each evaluator caught.
  • A regression test bench reruns every known case before any change is kept.
  • Fictional tenders in US units, in metric units and euros, in Spanish priced the Chilean way, and an Arabic page, each with known faults.
  • Measurement plans fixed before any pilot data is seen.
  • For a tender already evaluated: the earlier panel's result is entered after the close, and code compares rankings, scores, issues and time against thresholds fixed in advance.
  • The earlier panel's scores can be filled from its own spreadsheet; code matches columns and bidders, and the person checks every figure.
Measured on published public evaluations · Set against real panels' scores on eight published public evaluations.
  • Criteria and points of four public solicitations read correctly, and every obligation on an independent hand list found.
  • Rankings compared with real panels: rank correlation 0.88 to 0.92 on eight consultants; 0.725 on 13 firms; close to nil where five firms were nearly indistinguishable.
  • A repeat run moves a criterion score by at most two points; ranking correlation 0.96 between runs.
  • On a state transportation department's tabulation, all 368 line amounts and three totals agreed to the cent; on a panel's own scoresheet, 106 of 108 calculations agreed.
  • The arithmetic check caught an AI reading that counted one criterion twice.
  • A fifth case, four shortlisted firms: of the two the panel interviewed, Evalia's written scores already put the eventual winner ahead, as the panel did only after the interviews.
  • A sixth case, eleven firms: rank correlation 0.42 with the committee, inconclusive; the same top two firms, in reverse order.
  • A seventh case, six firms: rank correlation 0.55, inconclusive; 23 of 24 criterion scores within the seven evaluators' range.
  • An eighth case, four firms: 13 of 16 criterion scores within the five members' range, and all 16 on the committee's own scoring steps.
