Results as of 11 November 2026

Tested on five real public evaluations

Evalia was run on five public tender evaluations, with 36 firms in all, using the owners' own published documents and scores. Its ranking agreed with the panel's in two cases and was inconclusive in a third; in the other two there were too few firms, or the firms were too close to one another, to judge. In the first two cases, code checks caught errors that went unremarked, and every result can be verified from the documents alone.

What was tested

All five cases are public records: the solicitation, every submission and each evaluator's scores were published by the owner. The model read one submission at a time and never saw the panel's scores. The measures and the thresholds for "agreement" were written down before any model run. Firms and evaluators are not named here.

Interpretation Evalia's agreement with individual evaluators falls within the range of the evaluators' agreement with each other. It behaves like a strict additional evaluator, not like an outlier. Where it differs, it says why: in case B, it marks the eventual winner down for key roles the solicitation required but the submission did not name, and for a risk plan that leaves out risks beyond the contractor's control, which an addendum asked for. Whether those readings are right needs a reading of the pages it cites.

What the checks by code found

Arithmetic, dates, signatures and quotations are checked by code, never by the model. On real documents these checks found:

  • Fact A price error. One finalist's price form stated a fee $154 higher than its own percentage of the estimated construction cost gives, and a total $154 too high. The owner scored the corrected figures without recording the correction. Evalia found it without being told of it.
  • Fact The panel's own arithmetic. Of 108 recomputations of the published scoresheet (weighted scores, totals, ranks, rank sums, interview totals and averages), 106 agree. The two differences, a blank cell worth zero and a column heading that names the same firm twice, change no outcome.
  • Fact Unsigned forms. Required forms left unsigned on scanned pages of two real submissions, which the published scores did not reflect.
  • Fact An AI reading error. Reading a city's solicitation, the AI counted one criterion twice, once whole and once through its two parts, giving 115 points against the stated 100. The arithmetic check flagged it; the solicitation itself was correct.
  • Fact A missed requirement. A firm formed in October 2022 bidding against a ten-year experience requirement: one evaluator marked it down, two did not.

Anyone can verify the evaluation

Each evaluation produces an audit file: the fingerprint of every document, every calculation and every model call. A separate check, run with only the documents and that file, needs no access to Evalia and no model. It confirms four things:

  • the model was given exactly these documents, because each scoring prompt, rebuilt from the documents, matches the one recorded;
  • every score in a report is the model's recorded answer, unedited;
  • every quote is on the page it cites;
  • every calculation is right.

Fact On cases A and B the check reproduced all 36 scoring prompts and score records and all 454 quotes, with no differences. On altered copies it reported every change made, including a score raised by one point with the total adjusted to hide it.

Limits

  • Limit Five cases and 36 firms rule out gross disagreement with a panel; they do not show how close Evalia's scores sit to a typical panel's. Three of the five come from one state agency's forms, and case E covers only the four shortlisted firms of ten.
  • Limit Evalia scores more strictly than these panels did: its totals are lower even where its order agrees.
  • Limit The interviews that decided case B are not public and could not be tested.
  • Limit The audit file shows that its parts agree with each other and with the documents. It does not show when it was made; recording its fingerprint with the owner at the time would.
  • Limit In case E, six of nine compliance flags were at first wrong: the check applied the original solicitation where an addendum had changed it. After the fix on 11 November 2026, a rerun raised none of those six; six flags remain for a person to judge.
  • Limit No client has used Evalia yet.
